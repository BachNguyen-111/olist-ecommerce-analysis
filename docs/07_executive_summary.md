# Executive Summary: Olist Delivery & Customer Experience

**To:** Head of Operations, Olist (simulated) · **Data:** 96,478 delivered orders, Sep 2016 – Aug 2018

## The answer

**Late delivery is the biggest controllable driver of bad reviews. The delay happens in long-distance carrier transit, and it is concentrated in seven states that buy almost entirely from out-of-state sellers.** Fixing carrier performance and delivery estimates on those routes, starting with Rio de Janeiro, is the highest-return logistics investment.

## Key findings

| What we found | Why it matters | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| On-time orders average **4.29 stars**; orders 8+ days late average **1.70**. Late orders are 6.7% of reviewed orders but **32.5% of all 1–2 star reviews** | Lateness is the clearest driver of bad reviews | Treat on-time delivery as the top customer-experience KPI |
| **Seven states** (MA, CE, BA, RJ, PA, ES, PB) are below 90% on time. They place 21.8% of orders but get **39.3% of late orders** | The problem is regional, not national (Brazil overall: 93.2%) | Fix carriers and estimated dates in these states first |
| **Rio de Janeiro** is 12.8% of orders but **22.9% of late orders** (1,495 orders) | Largest single source of late deliveries | Start with RJ |
| Late orders spend **27.9 days with the carrier vs 7.9** for on-time orders; seller handling adds only 3 days | The bottleneck is carrier transit, not sellers | Review carrier contracts and performance on the slowest routes |
| **64% of orders cross state lines**, and those orders are late 8.0% of the time vs 4.5% within a state. They make up **76% of late orders**. In the seven worst states, 92–100% of orders come from out of state | Distance from São Paulo, where 71% of items are sold, drives lateness | Recruit sellers or stock closer to Northeast and Rio customers |
| Late orders were **promised the same window** as on-time ones (23.3 vs 24.5 days) | Estimates don't reflect the real route | Set estimates by seller-to-customer route |
| Late rate jumped from a usual 1–8% to **12.4% in Nov 2017** (Black Friday), **14.1% in Feb 2018** and **19.0% in Mar 2018** | Peaks break the network | Add carrier capacity and extend estimates before known peaks |
| Only **3.0%** of customers ever order again | Almost all revenue comes from new customers, so a bad first delivery loses the customer | After the delivery fix, test a second-order offer for customers whose first order arrived on time |
| Revenue grew **6.6x during 2017** to a Nov 2017 peak of R$1.15M, then **plateaued near R$1.06M a month** in 2018 | Growth has stalled | Retention, not just acquisition, is now the lever |

## Recommendations, in priority order

1. **Bring the seven worst states up to the national on-time rate.** If they matched Brazil's 93.2%, about **1,140 fewer orders** would arrive late (17.4% of all late orders) over the period analyzed. Start with RJ, then the Northeast (MA, CE, BA).
2. **Fix the long-haul carrier leg.** Compare carriers on cross-state routes out of São Paulo, and move volume to the ones that deliver on time.
3. **Set delivery estimates by route.** Today's promise barely changes between orders that arrive on time and orders that don't. Estimates should use the seller's state, the customer's state and the season.
4. **Plan for peaks.** Add carrier capacity and extend estimates ahead of Black Friday and other known high-volume periods.
5. **Recover late orders proactively.** Notify customers when an order is running late and offer a small voucher, because 79% of orders arriving 8+ days late end with a 1–2 star review. Late orders carried R$1.15M of revenue (7.5%).
6. **Then grow repeat purchases.** Test a post-delivery offer targeted at customers with an on-time first order.

## Limitations

- **Correlation, not causation.** Late orders may also differ in product, seller or season. A controlled test (for example, new estimates in one state) would confirm the effect.
- **Delivered orders only**, purchased Sep 2016 – Aug 2018. Canceled orders are excluded.
- **Revenue = item price + freight**, not payments, so it leaves out installment interest (a 0.02% difference).
- **Carrier names aren't in the data**, so the carrier finding is about the transit stage as a whole, not individual carriers.

## Next steps

- Model delivery time by seller state, customer state and month to propose new estimates.
- Get carrier IDs from the logistics team, so carriers can be compared on the same routes.
- Pilot route-based delivery estimates in Rio de Janeiro and measure the change in review scores.
- Track on-time rate and review score monthly on the dashboard's Delivery page.
